\begin{abstract}
Chronic kidney disease (CKD) is an escalating global public health priority affecting more than 850 million individuals worldwide. While supervised machine learning (ML) models demonstrate considerable discriminative capacity for risk stratification, clinical translation is severely hindered by the ``black-box'' opacity of complex classifiers, documented instability of post-hoc explanation algorithms (e.g., SHAP) under sample resampling, and uncharacterized sensitivity to missing clinical data. Furthermore, retrospective biomedical ML studies frequently suffer from tautological target leakage by including direct diagnostic criteria (e.g., eGFR or albuminuria) as predictive features. In this study, we address these challenges using the Continuous National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES) 2021--2023 cycle. We audit and verify a cohort of $N = 6,326$ participants ($\ge 12$ years) with complete KDIGO 2024 clinical staging ($3,592$ non-CKD [$56.78\%$], $2,734$ CKD [$43.22\%$]), strictly excluding $5,607$ untested pediatric/unmeasured records with unknown stage. Seven target-defining variables are eliminated to prevent target leakage, retaining $21$ non-leaking predictors. Models are trained using Repeated Stratified 5-Fold Cross-Validation ($25$ folds). TreeSHAP and LinearSHAP attributions are computed on held-out test partitions, aggregated back to parent clinical variables, and evaluated across all $\binom{25}{2} = 300$ pairwise fold combinations via Spearman rank correlation ($\rho$) and Top-$k$ Jaccard similarity. Controlled Missing Completely at Random (MCAR) stress testing is conducted at $10\%$ and $20\%$ masking levels. Across 25 folds, XGBoost achieved an ROC-AUC of $0.8456 \pm 0.0129$ (PR-AUC $0.8246 \pm 0.0149$), closely matched by Logistic Regression ($0.8441 \pm 0.0123$) and Random Forest ($0.8406 \pm 0.0123$). Global SHAP analysis identified chronological age, blood urea nitrogen (BUN), systolic blood pressure, serum uric acid, and diagnosed diabetes as top consensus risk factors. Pairwise SHAP attribution stability across 300 fold resamples was exceptionally high for Random Forest (Spearman $\rho = 0.9652 \pm 0.0148$, Top-5 Jaccard $1.0000 \pm 0.0000$) and XGBoost ($\rho = 0.9414 \pm 0.0214$, Top-5 Jaccard $1.0000 \pm 0.0000$). Under $10\%$ and $20\%$ missingness, all models exhibited graceful degradation (ROC-AUC drop $\le 0.026$) while preserving high explanation ranking fidelity ($\rho \ge 0.931$--$0.975$). These results provide empirical best practices for leakage-safe algorithmic auditing and explainability verification in computational nephrology.

\keywords{Chronic Kidney Disease \and Explainable Artificial Intelligence \and SHAP \and Feature Attribution Stability \and Missing Data Sensitivity \and NHANES \and Soft Computing.}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Chronic kidney disease (CKD) is an escalating global health emergency characterized by progressive decline in renal function, affecting over 10\% to 15\% of the adult population worldwide ($>850$ million individuals)~\cite{kdigo2024,bikbov2020}. According to international consensus guidelines established by Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes (KDIGO 2024), CKD is clinically diagnosed by an estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) below $60\text{ mL/min/1.73 m}^2$ or persistent albuminuria (urinary albumin-to-creatinine ratio $[\text{UACR}] \ge 30\text{ mg/g}$) persisting for more than three months~\cite{kdigo2024,inker2021}.

Because early-stage CKD is predominantly asymptomatic, patients frequently remain undiagnosed until advanced, irreversible nephron loss has occurred~\cite{kovesdy2022}. Consequently, there is intense clinical interest in leveraging machine learning (ML) and soft computing techniques to perform automated risk stratification from routine electronic health records (EHR)~\cite{he2025,song2024}.

\subsection{Methodological Challenges in Medical Machine Learning}
Despite promising results, clinical translation of ML in nephrology is impeded by four critical vulnerabilities:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Tautological Target Leakage}: Numerous studies mistakenly include the very laboratory variables used to derive the clinical outcome---such as serum creatinine, eGFR, or spot urine albumin---directly in the predictor matrix~\cite{moons2025probast}. When a model is supplied with serum creatinine or eGFR to predict CKD presence, it simply reconstructs the deterministic formula of the KDIGO definition ($\text{eGFR} < 60$), yielding artificially inflated performance metrics ($\text{ROC-AUC} > 0.99$) that convey zero clinical utility.
    \item \textbf{`Black-Box' Opacity}: Complex ensemble models construct high-dimensional interaction manifolds that clinicians cannot intuitively trace without post-hoc explainability frameworks~\cite{lundberg2017}.
    \item \textbf{Attribution Instability Across Resampled Cohorts}: A critical vulnerability of explainable AI (XAI) is explanation instability: small perturbations in the training data resample induce substantial fluctuations in feature importance rankings~\cite{nandy2021,han2022}.
    \item \textbf{Missing-Data Sensitivity}: Routine clinical datasets are characterized by pervasive missingness~\cite{collins2024tripod}. Model resilience under missing-data stress is rarely quantified systematically.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Materials and Methods}
\subsection{Study Cohort and KDIGO Staging Criteria}
This investigation analyzed cross-sectional data from the 2021--2023 Continuous NHANES cycle administered by the CDC/NCHS~\cite{cdc2024}. In the raw secondary dataset ($N = 11,933$ participants, $29$ variables), kidney function evaluation is governed by KDIGO 2024 guidelines using the race-free 2021 CKD-EPI creatinine equation~\cite{inker2021} and UACR.

During data auditing, we discovered that $5,607$ participants who did not undergo kidney function laboratory testing (predominantly pediatric participants $<12$ years old) had \texttt{ckd\_stage = 'Unknown'} and were erroneously labeled as CKD-positive by the naive condition \texttt{(ckd\_stage != 'No CKD')}. Excluding these untested records produced a verified study cohort of $N = 6,326$ participants ($3,592$ No CKD [$56.78\%$], $2,734$ CKD [$43.22\%$]).

\subsection{Feature Policy and Leakage Elimination}
To prevent tautological target reconstruction, seven direct diagnostic features were quarantined: \texttt{participant\_id}, \texttt{egfr}, \texttt{serum\_creatinine}, \texttt{urine\_albumin}, \texttt{urine\_creatinine}, \texttt{albumin\_creatinine\_ratio}, and \texttt{ckd\_stage}. The approved feature matrix comprises $21$ non-leaking predictors: 14 continuous (age, BMI, weight, height, systolic BP, diastolic BP, BUN, serum albumin, phosphorus, bicarbonate, calcium, uric acid, poverty-income ratio, education level) and 7 categorical (gender, ethnicity, diagnosed diabetes, insulin use, diabetes pills, smoking history, current smoking status). Baseline characteristics are summarized in Table~\ref{tab:baseline}.

\subsection{Machine Learning Pipeline and Validation Protocol}
All transformations and evaluations were integrated within a Repeated Stratified 5-Fold Cross-Validation protocol with $5$ independent random repeats ($25$ total folds). Continuous features were processed using median imputation and z-score standard scaling. Categorical features were imputed using mode imputation and one-hot encoding. Crucially, all imputers, encoders, and scalers were fit strictly on the training partition of each fold ($N \approx 5,060$) and applied via transform to the held-out test fold ($N \approx 1,266$). We benchmarked three contrasting architectures: regularized Logistic Regression (L2, $C=1.0$), Random Forest ($300$ trees, \texttt{min\_samples\_leaf=2}), and XGBoost ($300$ trees, \texttt{max\_depth=3}, $\eta=0.05$).

\subsection{Explainability, Dummy Aggregation, and Stability Quantification}
Post-hoc local explanations were computed for all held-out validation instances using Shapley Additive Explanations (SHAP)~\cite{lundberg2017} via exact TreeSHAP for tree ensembles and LinearSHAP for Logistic Regression. One-hot dummy level attributions were aggregated back to parent clinical features by summing absolute Shapley contributions across levels:
\begin{equation}
\text{Attribution}_i(F) = \sum_{k \in \text{Levels}(F)} |\phi_{i, k}|
\end{equation}
Stability across all 300 cross-validation fold pairs was evaluated via pairwise Spearman rank correlation ($\rho$) and Top-$k$ Jaccard similarity indices ($k \in \{5, 10\}$).

\subsection{Controlled Missing-Data Stress Testing}
Evaluating model pipelines under synthetic MCAR masking demonstrated remarkable resilience and graceful degradation (Table~\ref{tab:missingness}). At 10\% additional missingness, ROC-AUC declined by only $\approx 0.011$--$0.013$ points across all models. Even under extreme 20\% additional missingness, all architectures maintained strong discriminative capacity ($\text{ROC-AUC} \ge 0.8207$). Crucially, SHAP feature rankings remained exceptionally stable under missingness stress: Random Forest maintained $\rho = 0.9727 \pm 0.0049$ relative to baseline explanations.

\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
In this comprehensive machine learning study utilizing the NHANES 2021--2023 cohort, we established that machine learning models achieve robust discrimination ($\text{ROC-AUC} \approx 0.846$, $\text{PR-AUC} \approx 0.825$) for concurrent CKD classification using exclusively pre-diagnostic systemic risk factors, completely independent of direct filtration markers. Global SHAP attributions independently validated known pathophysiological mechanisms: age-related nephron senescence~\cite{denic2016}, BUN filtration clearance, systolic vascular load and barotrauma~\cite{chobanian2003}, hyperuricemia-mediated intrarenal resistance~\cite{johnson2018}, and diabetic microvascular injury. Furthermore, TreeSHAP explanations for bagged and boosted tree ensembles achieve near-perfect ranking stability ($\rho > 0.94$--$0.96$; Top-5 Jaccard $= 1.000$) across resampled cross-validation folds and exhibit graceful degradation under missing data. These findings establish empirical best practices for leakage-safe algorithmic auditing and explainability verification in computational nephrology.
